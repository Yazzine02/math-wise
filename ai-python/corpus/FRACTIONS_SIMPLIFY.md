# Simplifying Fractions

A fraction is in its **simplest form** (or "lowest terms") when the numerator and denominator share no common factor greater than 1. Simplifying means dividing both top and bottom by their **greatest common divisor (GCD)** until they have no factors in common.

## The simplification procedure

To simplify 8/12:
1. Find the GCD of 8 and 12. The factors of 8 are 1, 2, 4, 8. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The largest factor they share is **4**.
2. Divide both numerator and denominator by the GCD: 8 ÷ 4 = **2**, 12 ÷ 4 = **3**.
3. Simplified form: **2/3**.

You can also simplify in steps if you don't immediately see the GCD: 8/12 → divide by 2 → 4/6 → divide by 2 again → 2/3. Same result, just slower. Stop when the numerator and denominator share no factor greater than 1.

## Prime factorization as a fallback

When the GCD isn't obvious, break each number into its prime factors. For 8/12: 8 = 2 × 2 × 2 and 12 = 2 × 2 × 3. They share two factors of 2, so the GCD is 4. Cancel the shared 2s: (2 × 2 × 2) / (2 × 2 × 3) = **2/3**.

## Common student errors

- **Subtracting instead of dividing**: writing 8/12 → (8-4)/(12-4) = 4/8 = 1/2. Wrong — simplification divides, it does not subtract.
- **Dividing top and bottom by different numbers**: 8/12 → divide top by 2 and bottom by 3, giving 4/4 = 1. Wrong — the same divisor must be applied to both.
- **Stopping too early**: 8/12 → 4/6, then declaring it simplified. The numerator and denominator still share a factor of 2.
- **Reducing across operations**: in 5 + 8/12, trying to simplify the 5 against the 12. The 5 isn't a numerator of the fraction; only the fraction itself simplifies.

The diagnostic shortcut: if the simplified fraction is still reducible, the student stopped early. If the answer has an unexpected pattern (like equal numerator and denominator), they likely divided top and bottom by different numbers.
