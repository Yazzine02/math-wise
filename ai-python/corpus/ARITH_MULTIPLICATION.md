# Multiplication of Whole Numbers

Multiplication is repeated addition: 7 × 8 means adding seven groups of eight (or eight groups of seven — multiplication is **commutative**). The numbers being multiplied are the **factors**; the result is the **product**.

## Single-digit products — the times tables

Single-digit multiplication should be recalled from memory. Slips here propagate into every multi-digit problem. The classic confusions are: 7×8 = 56 (often mis-recalled as 54 or 58), 6×9 = 54, 8×7 = 56, 9×7 = 63.

## Multi-digit multiplication with carrying

For two-digit by one-digit (e.g. 47 × 6):
1. Multiply the units digit: 7 × 6 = **42**. Write **2** in the units column, carry **4** to the tens column.
2. Multiply the tens digit and **add the carry**: 4 × 6 = 24, plus the carried **4** gives **28**.
3. Write **28** to the left of the **2**. Final product: **282**.

The most error-prone step is the "add the carry **after** multiplying" sequencing. Students often add the carry first (4 + 4 = 8, then 8 × 6 = 48 → wrong product).

## Common student errors

- **Carry-then-multiply order error**: adding the carry to the digit before multiplying, instead of after. Produces an answer that's roughly proportional but wrong.
- **Forgetting the carry entirely**: 47 × 6 → writes just "242" (the 4×6=24 result with the units digit 2 stuck on, no carry added). Result is 40 too small.
- **Times-table slip**: the multi-digit procedure is followed perfectly but a single small product is wrong (7×8 said as 54). Result is off by a multiple of 10 or 100 depending on where the slip happened.
- **Sign error on signed multiplication**: a negative times a negative gives a positive; mis-applying this rule flips the sign of the answer.

The diagnostic shortcut: if the result is roughly the right size but the units digit is wrong, it's a times-table slip in the units multiplication. If the units digit matches but the tens region is wrong by a small amount, the carry was mishandled.
