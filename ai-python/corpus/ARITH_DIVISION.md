# Division of Whole Numbers

Division splits a number (the **dividend**) into equal-sized groups defined by the **divisor**, producing the **quotient**. The question "what is 84 ÷ 7?" asks how many sevens fit into 84.

## Division as the inverse of multiplication

Every division has a corresponding multiplication: 84 ÷ 7 = 12 is the same statement as 12 × 7 = 84. The fastest way to check a division answer is to multiply it back by the divisor — you should land on the original dividend.

## The long division procedure

For 156 ÷ 12:
1. Look at the first digit of the dividend (1). 12 does not fit into 1, so look at the first two digits (15). 12 fits into 15 once, with remainder 3.
2. Write 1 above the 5 in the quotient. Subtract 12 from 15 to get 3.
3. Bring down the next digit (6) next to the 3 → 36. 12 fits into 36 exactly 3 times.
4. Write 3 above the 6 in the quotient. Subtract 36 from 36 to get 0. No remainder.
5. Quotient: **13**.

## Common student errors

- **Times-table slip embedded in the division**: estimating that 12 fits into 36 four times (giving a remainder of -12, a clear sign something went wrong) or two times (giving a remainder of 12, which is larger than the divisor — also a clear sign). Either way, the underlying error is a multiplication recall slip, not a division-procedure error.
- **Forgetting the remainder**: writing the quotient ignoring a non-zero remainder when the problem expected an integer answer, or writing a quotient when the division doesn't divide evenly. For 17 ÷ 5, the answer is 3 remainder 2 (or 3.4), not just 3.
- **Sign error on signed division**: a negative divided by a negative gives a positive; mis-applying gives the wrong sign.
- **Dividing by zero**: producing "undefined" as a number rather than recognizing it as illegal.

The diagnostic shortcut: when the quotient is off by 1, suspect a partial-multiplication error inside the long-division step. When the answer is fractional but the student wrote an integer, they likely truncated instead of giving the remainder. Always cross-check by multiplying quotient × divisor — if it doesn't return the dividend, the division is wrong.
