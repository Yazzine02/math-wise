# Subtraction of Whole Numbers

Subtraction takes one number (the **subtrahend**) away from another (the **minuend**), producing the **difference**. For multi-digit numbers, align the digits by place value and subtract column by column starting from the units.

## The borrowing procedure (regrouping)

When the top digit in a column is **smaller than the bottom digit**, you cannot subtract directly. You **borrow** 1 from the next column to the left: that 1 represents ten units of the current column. The next column's top digit decreases by 1, the current column's top digit increases by 10, and then the subtraction proceeds normally.

Worked example — 73 - 28:
1. Units column: 3 - 8 — can't be done as is. Borrow from the tens: the **7** in the tens column becomes **6**, and the **3** in the units column becomes **13**.
2. Units column (after borrow): 13 - 8 = **5**.
3. Tens column: 6 - 2 = **4**.
4. Final difference: **45**.

## Common student errors

- **Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger digit in each column, regardless of which is on top**: for 73 - 28, computing 8 - 3 = 5 in the units column instead of borrowing. The result is 55 instead of 45. This is the single most common subtraction error.
- **Forgetting to decrement after borrowing**: borrowing for the units column but leaving the tens column unchanged. Result is 10 too large.
- **Cascading borrow errors**: when borrowing from a column whose digit is 0 (e.g. 500 - 27), failing to propagate the borrow all the way through. This is essentially "borrowing from a borrow".
- **Sign confusion**: writing the answer as a negative number when the minuend was actually larger than the subtrahend.

The diagnostic shortcut: if the student's answer is exactly 10 too large, they probably forgot to decrement after borrowing. If a units digit looks "flipped" relative to the correct one, they likely subtracted small-from-large by habit instead of borrowing.
