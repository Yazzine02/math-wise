# Adding and Subtracting Fractions

You cannot add or subtract fractions unless they share the same **denominator**. Otherwise you'd be adding "pieces of different sizes" — like trying to add 3 quarters and 2 thirds by saying "5 of something". The procedure is to rewrite each fraction over a **common denominator**, then add or subtract the numerators.

## The procedure with a common denominator

To compute 1/4 + 1/4:
1. Denominators already match (both are 4). Add the numerators: 1 + 1 = **2**.
2. Keep the denominator: result is **2/4**.
3. Simplify: 2/4 = **1/2**.

The key rule: **never add denominators**. The denominator names the size of the pieces, not their count.

## Finding a common denominator

For 1/3 + 1/4:
1. Find a common multiple of 3 and 4. The lowest common multiple (LCM) is **12**.
2. Rewrite each fraction over 12: 1/3 = 4/12 (multiply top and bottom by 4) and 1/4 = 3/12 (multiply top and bottom by 3).
3. Add the numerators: 4 + 3 = **7**.
4. Keep the denominator: result is **7/12**. Already simplified.

When you don't see the LCM immediately, the product of the two denominators always works as a common denominator (slower, may need extra simplification at the end).

## Common student errors

- **Adding both numerators and denominators**: 1/3 + 1/4 → 2/7. This is the single most common fraction error. The student is treating the fractions like ordered pairs being added componentwise. The fix is to convert to a common denominator FIRST, then only add numerators.
- **Subtracting denominators**: same shape of error, applied to subtraction: 1/2 - 1/3 → 0/(-1). Equally wrong.
- **Forgetting to convert the numerator when changing the denominator**: rewriting 1/3 as 1/12 (changed the denominator but not the numerator). The fraction has now silently shrunk to a quarter of its original value.
- **Sign errors on subtraction with mixed-magnitude fractions**: 1/4 - 1/2 = -1/4, but writing 1/4 instead.

The diagnostic shortcut: if the denominator of the student's answer is the sum or product of the original denominators with no conversion of numerators, they added numerators and denominators directly. Direct them to find the common denominator first.
