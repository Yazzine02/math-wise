# Addition of Whole Numbers

Addition combines two or more numbers (the **addends**) into a single total (the **sum**). For multi-digit numbers, align the digits by place value — units under units, tens under tens, hundreds under hundreds — and add column by column starting from the units (rightmost) column.

## The carrying procedure

When the sum of a column is **10 or more**, write down the units digit of that sum and **carry** the tens digit into the next column to the left. The carried 1 (or 2, in rare cases) is added to the next column's total before you write its result.

Worked example — 47 + 38:
1. Units column: 7 + 8 = **15**. Write **5**, carry **1** into the tens column.
2. Tens column: 4 + 3 + **1** (carried) = **8**. Write **8**.
3. Final sum: **85**.

## Common student errors

- **Forgetting to carry**: writing 47 + 38 = 75 (added each column independently but ignored the 15 → kept just the 5 and the 4+3=7, dropping the carry). The give-away is that the units digit is correct but the answer is exactly 10 less than the true sum.
- **Carrying the wrong digit**: when the column sums to 15, carrying the 5 and writing the 1, instead of the reverse. The result is wildly off.
- **Misaligned digits**: for numbers of different widths (47 + 8), forgetting to right-align so the 8 ends up in the tens column. The result is roughly 10× too large.
- **Adding left-to-right without carrying**: producing concatenated digits like 47 + 38 = "715" by summing 4+3=7 and 7+8=15 and gluing them. The result has too many digits.

The diagnostic shortcut: if the result is exactly 10 less than the true sum, the student forgot a carry. If it's roughly 10× off, the digits were misaligned. If the units digit matches but the rest is wrong, the carry direction was reversed.
